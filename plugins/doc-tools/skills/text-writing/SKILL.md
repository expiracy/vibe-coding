# Text Writing

Write for the reader's purpose. Follow the requested audience, voice, format,
length, and spelling conventions; preserve established document requirements.

## Build a clear narrative

Lead with the main point, conclusion, decision, or request. Follow with the
context and evidence the reader needs to understand and assess it. Connect
paragraphs so each develops one idea and prepares the reader for the next.

For explanatory documents, tell the story of the need, the approach, its
implications, and any next step. Use that arc to organize the explanation;
it need not become a fixed set of headings. Explain mechanisms through their
purpose, effect, and relationships rather than recounting implementation work.

Keep the main explanation at a high level unless a detail helps the reader
understand the approach, assess a claim or limitation, or make a decision.
Link existing supporting material when useful. Retain the necessary detail in
specifications, reference material, and procedures, including exact conditions
and actionable steps.

## Keep the meaning intact

Preserve facts, names, numbers, dates, conditions, uncertainty, attribution, and
important limitations. Distinguish proposals from implemented behavior and
expected benefits from observed results. Do not turn limited testing into a
claim of proven success or invent missing evidence to make a narrative smoother.

Keep quotations, citations, code, and identifiers accurate when editing the
surrounding prose. Resolve material ambiguities with the user or state the
assumption when the task permits it. Preserve the actual request or commitment.

## Make the prose easy to follow

Use concrete nouns, familiar words, and precise verbs. Prefer active voice when
the actor is known. Keep necessary technical terms and explain unfamiliar ones
for the intended audience.

Remove filler, repeated conclusions, inflated claims, and forced enthusiasm.
Use connected prose for explanations, lists for parallel items or steps, and
tables for comparisons. Use headings to help readers navigate longer documents.

Before delivering, check that the main point is easy to find, the reasoning
flows, the important qualifications remain, and any next action is clear.
